# Offset Abide

**For the HIPAA Security Rule and Breach Notification Rule.** Do the risk
analysis, decide the addressable specifications and write down why, and be
ready to show OCR the paperwork. On your own server.

## Who it is for

Covered entities and business associates holding electronic patient data:
practices, clinics, billing companies, and the software and service vendors
who sign a BAA. It suits an organisation without a security team, and the
consultants who help them.

> HIPAA has no certification. No product can make you compliant, and nobody
> can certify you. What this does is the work, and the records behind it.

## What you get

| | |
|---|---|
| **The Rules** | 72 requirements in 6 parts: all 29 standards and the 43 implementation specifications from 45 CFR 164, Subparts C and D |
| **Required and addressable** | Every specification marked as the Rule marks it — 21 required, 22 addressable — and an addressable one you decide against has to carry the reason |
| **The addressable decisions** | The record OCR asks for: what you decided on each addressable specification, why, and what you did instead. As a PDF |
| **Guidance on every requirement** | What it means, what to do, what an investigator looks for, and what proof to keep. Plain English, not the text of the regulation |
| **Get ready** | A plan of 6 stages and 29 steps, starting with the risk analysis because that is what OCR asks for first. 20 steps tick themselves from your data |
| **Sample library** | 80 assets and 40 risks drawn from healthcare, covering every standard, to add with one click |
| **Registers** | Evidence, risks, assets, policies, tasks, incidents and findings, plus suppliers, training, objectives, interested parties, and audits and reviews |
| **Business associates** | The Suppliers register holds each vendor, its agreement, what data it touches, and when the BAA was last reviewed |
| **Breach notification** | Guidance on the 60-day clock, the individual, media and Secretary notices, the small-breach log kept for the year, and a vendor's duty to tell you |
| **Corrective action** | Findings with root cause, action taken and verification |
| **Control testing** | A dated record of each test and its result, to show a safeguard works over time |
| **Calendar and gap analysis** | Everything due in one list, and what is left or claimed without proof |
| **Reports (PDF, with your logo)** | Executive summary, addressable decisions, gap report, readiness plan, management review pack, document control list, risk register, evidence register |
| **Spreadsheets** | CSV export and import on every register |
| **Help and Documentation** | 11 pages in the product, including a HIPAA playbook and what to do on the first day of a suspected breach |

## Every Offset product also has

HTTPS with your certificate · four roles · every change recorded in an audit
trail that an investigator can read and download · nightly backups and restore
from the screen · signed one-click updates · email reminders and a daily
digest · Forgot password for administrators.

## How it installs

| | |
|---|---|
| **Windows** | One installer. Nothing else to install |
| **Linux** | A package that installs as a system service. Ubuntu, Debian, RHEL, Amazon Linux, SUSE |
| **Docker** | Pull the image and run it with Compose |
| **Needs** | A 64-bit server, about 300 MB of disk plus your evidence, and one port. It uses under 150 MB of memory at rest |
| **Sized for** | Up to about 50 users |

## Your data stays yours

It runs on your server, inside your network. No cloud, no telemetry, no
licence server. Offset Security has no access to it, and no patient data ever
leaves the machine. Details are in the security overview and the privacy
sheet.
