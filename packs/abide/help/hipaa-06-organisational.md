# Organisational requirements (164.314)

The organisational requirements say what your agreements with other organisations must contain: business associate agreements, and, if you sponsor a group health plan, the plan documents.

164.308(b) says you need an agreement with every business associate. This section says what it must say. A business associate agreement must also meet the Privacy Rule's requirements in 164.504(e), which add terms such as returning or destroying the data when the contract ends. Have your agreements reviewed by someone qualified to do so.

Part of [the audit-ready handbook](hipaa-01-how-compliance-is-checked.md). Previous: [Technical safeguards](hipaa-05-technical.md). Next: [Policies and documentation](hipaa-07-documentation.md).

## How to read this page

Each **standard** is followed by its **implementation specifications**. Standards are always required. Each specification is marked **Required** or **Addressable**; see [the general rules](hipaa-02-general-rules.md) for what addressable asks of you. A standard with no specifications under it must be met as written.

The section numbers and titles are from 45 CFR Part 164. The wording under each is ours, not the regulation's.

## 164.314(a)(1) Business associate contracts or other arrangements

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** What the agreement with a vendor handling patient data has to contain.

**What to do.** Make sure each agreement says the vendor will safeguard the data, will make its subcontractors do the same, and will report security incidents, including breaches, to you. The Privacy Rule, 164.504(e), adds further terms, including returning or destroying the data at the end.

**What OCR looks for.** Agreements signed years ago that no longer match what the vendor actually does, or that never had breach reporting in them.

**Have ready.** Executed agreements; a review date against each; the register that ties them to vendors.

### 164.314(a)(2)(i) Business associate contracts (required)

**Purpose.** The specific promises the contract must extract from the vendor.

**What to do.** The agreement must require the vendor to: comply with the Security Rule's safeguards for ePHI; bind its subcontractors to the same; and report security incidents to you, including breaches of unsecured data. Put a stated timescale on the reporting. The Privacy Rule, 164.504(e), adds further terms, including return or destruction of the data at the end.

**What OCR looks for.** A missing reporting timescale. 'Promptly' is not a timescale - name the number of days.

**Have ready.** The clauses themselves, and the dates they were agreed.

### 164.314(a)(2)(ii) Other arrangements (required)

**Purpose.** What takes the place of a contract when both parties are government bodies.

**What to do.** If this is you, use a memorandum of understanding or the arrangement your law provides, and keep it filed like any other agreement.

**What OCR looks for.** Public-sector relationships running on nothing because a contract felt inappropriate.

**Have ready.** The memorandum or equivalent, signed and dated.

### 164.314(a)(2)(iii) Business associate contracts with subcontractors (required)

**Purpose.** A vendor's own suppliers are bound by the same terms.

**What to do.** If you are a business associate yourself, sign agreements with your subcontractors on the same terms. If you are a covered entity, ask your vendors to show that they have.

**What OCR looks for.** The cloud provider behind your vendor, with no agreement anywhere in the chain.

**Have ready.** Subcontractor agreements, or written confirmation from the vendor that they hold them.

## 164.314(b)(1) Requirements for group health plans

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** Where a group health plan shares data with the employer sponsoring it, the plan documents must say how that data is protected.

**What to do.** If you run a group health plan, amend the plan documents to require the sponsor to safeguard ePHI and to keep plan data away from employment decisions. If you do not, record that this does not apply.

**What OCR looks for.** Plan documents that were never amended, or a not-applicable claim with nothing behind it.

**Have ready.** Amended plan documents, or a documented decision that no group health plan exists.

### 164.314(b)(2) Implementation specifications for plan sponsors (required)

**Purpose.** What those plan documents have to require of the sponsor.

**What to do.** Require reasonable safeguards, separation between plan administration and employment functions, the same terms for anyone the sponsor passes data to, and reporting of incidents back to the plan.

**What OCR looks for.** Separation on paper that does not exist in practice, with the same HR staff seeing both.

**Have ready.** The plan document clauses; evidence of how the separation works day to day.
