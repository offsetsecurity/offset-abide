# Physical safeguards (164.310)

The physical safeguards protect the places, equipment and media that hold ePHI: who can get into the building and the equipment rooms, how workstations are used and protected, and how devices and media are tracked, reused and destroyed.

They apply wherever ePHI is: the server room, the reception desk, the laptop taken home, and the photocopier with a hard drive in it. If a vendor hosts your systems, their data centre is covered through your business associate agreement rather than your own locks.

Part of [the audit-ready handbook](hipaa-01-how-compliance-is-checked.md). Previous: [Administrative safeguards](hipaa-03-administrative.md). Next: [Technical safeguards](hipaa-05-technical.md).

## How to read this page

Each **standard** is followed by its **implementation specifications**. Standards are always required. Each specification is marked **Required** or **Addressable**; see [the general rules](hipaa-02-general-rules.md) for what addressable asks of you. A standard with no specifications under it must be met as written.

The section numbers and titles are from 45 CFR Part 164. The wording under each is ours, not the regulation's.

## 164.310(a)(1) Facility access controls

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** Limiting who can physically get to the rooms and equipment holding patient data.

**What to do.** Lock the server room and the wiring cupboard. Control who holds keys and badges. Know who was in the building, and escort visitors who go anywhere near the equipment.

**What OCR looks for.** Server equipment in an unlocked room, a cupboard everyone has a key to, or a comms rack in a public corridor.

**Have ready.** Key and badge register; door access logs; visitor log; photographs of how equipment is secured.

### 164.310(a)(2)(i) Contingency operations (addressable)

**Purpose.** Getting into the building to restore data after an emergency, without letting everyone else in.

**What to do.** Say who may enter during an emergency, how they get in if the badge system is down, and how that entry is recorded.

**What OCR looks for.** An emergency plan that assumes the doors work and the usual people are available.

**Have ready.** Emergency access procedure; named holders of override keys; records of emergency entry.

### 164.310(a)(2)(ii) Facility security plan (addressable)

**Purpose.** A written plan for protecting the building and its equipment from unauthorised physical access, tampering and theft.

**What to do.** Write down what protects each area - locks, alarms, cameras, reception - and who is responsible for each.

**What OCR looks for.** Whether the plan matches what is actually installed, especially after a move or refit.

**Have ready.** Facility security plan; alarm and camera coverage; maintenance records for them.

### 164.310(a)(2)(iii) Access control and validation procedures (addressable)

**Purpose.** Checking that a person is who they say they are before letting them into sensitive areas, including engineers and contractors.

**What to do.** Verify identity at reception, issue visitor badges, escort contractors in equipment rooms, and check that an engineer was actually sent by the company they claim.

**What OCR looks for.** Contractors with unescorted access, and visitor logs with gaps.

**Have ready.** Visitor and contractor procedures; sign-in records; escort arrangements.

### 164.310(a)(2)(iv) Maintenance records (addressable)

**Purpose.** A record of repairs and changes to the physical security of the building - locks, doors, walls, alarms.

**What to do.** Log it when a lock is changed, a door is repaired, or an alarm is serviced. Note what, when and who did it.

**What OCR looks for.** Changes to physical security nobody can account for.

**Have ready.** Maintenance log covering locks, doors, alarms and cameras.

## 164.310(b) Workstation use

**Standard.** No implementation specifications: the standard itself must be met.

**Purpose.** Rules for how workstations that can reach patient data are used, and what a proper environment for them looks like.

**What to do.** Say what staff may and may not do on a clinical workstation, including at home: no personal email for patient data, lock the screen when you walk away, no family members using it.

**What OCR looks for.** Home working arrangements with no rules written for them.

**Have ready.** Workstation use policy; acknowledgement by staff; remote working rules.

## 164.310(c) Workstation security

**Standard.** No implementation specifications: the standard itself must be met.

**Purpose.** Physically protecting the workstations themselves from people who should not use them.

**What to do.** Angle screens away from waiting areas, use privacy filters at reception, lock laptops to desks or lock them away, and set screens to lock quickly when idle.

**What OCR looks for.** Reception and nursing-station screens visible to patients. It is one of the first things anyone walking through will notice.

**Have ready.** Screen positions and privacy filters; automatic lock settings; laptop security arrangements.

## 164.310(d)(1) Device and media controls

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** Controlling the hardware and media holding patient data as it moves in, around and out of your organisation.

**What to do.** Keep a register of devices and removable media. Control how they leave the building, and how they are wiped or destroyed at the end.

**What OCR looks for.** Devices that went to disposal with no record of how the data on them was destroyed.

**Have ready.** Device register; movement records; disposal certificates.

### 164.310(d)(2)(i) Disposal (required)

**Purpose.** Getting rid of patient data, and the hardware it was on, so it cannot be recovered.

**What to do.** Wipe or physically destroy drives before anything leaves. Use a service that gives you a certificate listing serial numbers. Do not sell or donate equipment without it.

**What OCR looks for.** The certificate against the device register. Photocopiers and MFPs are the classic miss - they have hard drives.

**Have ready.** Destruction or wipe certificates with serial numbers; the register entry closed off.

### 164.310(d)(2)(ii) Media re-use (required)

**Purpose.** Removing patient data from media before it is used for something else.

**What to do.** Wipe a device before it is handed to another member of staff or another department. A quick delete or a reformat is not enough on its own.

**What OCR looks for.** Machines passed between staff with the previous user's data still on them.

**Have ready.** Wipe records tied to device and date; the standard you wipe to.

### 164.310(d)(2)(iii) Accountability (addressable)

**Purpose.** Knowing where the hardware and media went, and who had it.

**What to do.** Record moves of equipment holding ePHI: who took it, when, and where to. Include laptops taken home and drives sent off site.

**What OCR looks for.** A device register that has not been checked against reality for years.

**Have ready.** Movement log; periodic inventory check with discrepancies chased.

### 164.310(d)(2)(iv) Data backup and storage (addressable)

**Purpose.** Making a copy of patient data before you move equipment that holds it.

**What to do.** Back up a device before it is moved, upgraded or repaired, so a failure in transit does not lose the only copy.

**What OCR looks for.** Equipment moved or sent for repair with no copy taken first.

**Have ready.** Backup taken and verified before the move, noted against the device.
