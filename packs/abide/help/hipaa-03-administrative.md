# Administrative safeguards (164.308)

The administrative safeguards are the largest part of the Security Rule, and the part an investigator usually reads first. They cover how you manage security: the risk analysis and what you do about it, who is responsible, who gets access, training, incidents, contingency planning, evaluation, and the agreements with vendors who handle ePHI for you.

Everything starts with the risk analysis, 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A). It decides which addressable specifications are reasonable for you, and it is the document OCR most often finds missing or out of date.

Part of [the audit-ready handbook](hipaa-01-how-compliance-is-checked.md). Previous: [The general rules](hipaa-02-general-rules.md). Next: [Physical safeguards](hipaa-04-physical.md).

## How to read this page

Each **standard** is followed by its **implementation specifications**. Standards are always required. Each specification is marked **Required** or **Addressable**; see [the general rules](hipaa-02-general-rules.md) for what addressable asks of you. A standard with no specifications under it must be met as written.

The section numbers and titles are from 45 CFR Part 164. The wording under each is ours, not the regulation's.

## 164.308(a)(1)(i) Security management process

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** The duty to find the risks to patient data you hold, and to do something about them. Everything else in the Security Rule hangs off this one.

**What to do.** Run a risk analysis, act on what it finds, discipline staff who break the rules, and review your system activity. The four specifications under this standard are all required.

**What OCR looks for.** OCR routinely asks for this early in an investigation. They look for a current risk analysis and evidence that findings were actually addressed, not filed.

**Have ready.** Risk analysis report; risk treatment plan with owners and dates; sanction policy; activity review records.

### 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A) Risk analysis (required)

**Purpose.** A written assessment of where electronic patient data lives, what could go wrong with it, and how likely and how bad that would be.

**What to do.** List every system, device and service that creates, receives, stores or transmits ePHI - including laptops, phones, backups and anything a vendor hosts. Work through the threats to each, record likelihood and impact, and keep it current as things change.

**What OCR looks for.** One of the failures OCR cites most often in its settlements. They check it covers the whole environment, not just the EHR, and that it has been revisited since it was first written.

**Have ready.** Current risk analysis covering every system in the asset register; the date it was last reviewed; the previous version, to show it is maintained.

### 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(B) Risk management (required)

**Purpose.** Actually reducing the risks the analysis found, to a level you have decided is acceptable.

**What to do.** Turn each significant finding into a piece of work with an owner and a date. Track it to done. Where you accept a risk instead, say who accepted it and why.

**What OCR looks for.** Findings with no owner, no date and no progress. A risk analysis with nothing behind it is treated as no risk analysis at all.

**Have ready.** Risk treatment plan; closed items with evidence; accepted risks signed off by name.

### 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(C) Sanction policy (required)

**Purpose.** Written consequences for workforce members who break your security rules.

**What to do.** Write what happens, from a warning to dismissal, make it part of the staff handbook, and apply it when it happens. Keep a record of the times you did.

**What OCR looks for.** That the policy exists, that staff were told about it, and that it was used - snooping cases with no consequence are noticed.

**Have ready.** Sanction policy; signed acknowledgements; records of action taken, with names redacted if you prefer.

### 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(D) Information system activity review (required)

**Purpose.** Someone regularly looking at your logs, access reports and security incident tracking.

**What to do.** Decide what gets reviewed, how often and by whom - failed sign-ins, access to VIP records, after-hours activity. Do it on that schedule and write down what you saw and what you did about it.

**What OCR looks for.** Evidence of a review happening, not just logs existing. A log nobody reads is a finding.

**Have ready.** Review schedule; dated review notes; follow-up actions raised from them.

## 164.308(a)(2) Assigned security responsibility

**Standard.** No implementation specifications: the standard itself must be met.

**Purpose.** One named person accountable for security. Not a committee, not a job title nobody holds.

**What to do.** Name your Security Official in writing, make sure they know it, and say so where staff can see it. A small practice can name the office manager; the point is that someone owns it.

**What OCR looks for.** A name, a date, and a person who still works here and knows they hold the role. An appointment that outlived the person's employment is an easy finding.

**Have ready.** Written appointment; the name in your policies; the person's job description.

## 164.308(a)(3)(i) Workforce security

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** Making sure the right staff have access to patient data, and that everybody else does not.

**What to do.** Decide who needs access to what before you grant it, check people before you give it, and remove it the day they leave.

**What OCR looks for.** Leavers who still have accounts. It is quick to test and often finds something.

**Have ready.** Access approval records; screening records; a leavers list matched against account removals.

### 164.308(a)(3)(ii)(A) Authorization and/or supervision (addressable)

**Purpose.** Staff who can reach patient data are either authorised to do so or supervised while they do.

**What to do.** Have a manager approve access before IT grants it. For students, volunteers and temporary staff, say who supervises them.

**What OCR looks for.** Access granted on a verbal request, with no record of who approved it.

**Have ready.** Approval records naming the approver; supervision arrangements for trainees.

### 164.308(a)(3)(ii)(B) Workforce clearance procedure (addressable)

**Purpose.** Checking that someone is suitable before you give them access to patient data.

**What to do.** Decide what you check for which roles - identity, references, licence verification, background checks where your state allows - and do it before access is granted, not after.

**What OCR looks for.** That the check happened before the access, and that it is applied consistently rather than to some people only.

**Have ready.** Screening policy; completed checks on file; dates showing they preceded access.

### 164.308(a)(3)(ii)(C) Termination procedures (addressable)

**Purpose.** Cutting off access when someone leaves or changes job.

**What to do.** Make a checklist: disable accounts, collect the laptop and badge, change any shared passwords they knew, remove remote access. Trigger it from HR the same day, and do the same for a transfer, not only a leaver.

**What OCR looks for.** The gap between the leaving date and the account being disabled. Same-day is expected for a dismissal.

**Have ready.** Leaver checklist per person; account disable timestamps; asset return records.

## 164.308(a)(4)(i) Information access management

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** Rules for who gets access to what, written down, and followed every time access is granted or changed.

**What to do.** Define access by role. Grant the least a person needs to do their job. Review it periodically and when someone changes role.

**What OCR looks for.** Everyone having the same broad access because it was easier, especially clinical staff able to see every patient in the system.

**Have ready.** Role-to-access matrix; approval records; periodic access review results.

### 164.308(a)(4)(ii)(A) Isolating health care clearinghouse functions (required)

**Purpose.** If a clearinghouse is part of a larger organisation, it must keep its data away from the rest of the business.

**What to do.** If this applies, separate the clearinghouse systems and access from the parent organisation. If you are not a clearinghouse, record that this does not apply and why.

**What OCR looks for.** Either the separation, or a clear statement that the organisation performs no clearinghouse function.

**Have ready.** Network and access separation evidence, or a documented not-applicable decision.

### 164.308(a)(4)(ii)(B) Access authorization (addressable)

**Purpose.** How access to patient data gets approved in the first place.

**What to do.** Write down who may approve access to each system, and make the request go through them. Keep the approvals.

**What OCR looks for.** Who approved this person's access to this system, and when. Have the record ready.

**Have ready.** Access request and approval records, per person and per system.

### 164.308(a)(4)(ii)(C) Access establishment and modification (addressable)

**Purpose.** How access is set up, changed and reviewed over time.

**What to do.** Review who has access to what at a set interval - quarterly is common - and when people change role. Remove what is no longer needed and record the decision.

**What OCR looks for.** Accumulated access: the nurse who moved to billing and kept both. A review with no removals is treated as a review that did not happen.

**Have ready.** Dated access review records showing what was removed and by whom.

## 164.308(a)(5)(i) Security awareness and training

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** A security awareness and training programme for everyone in the workforce, including management.

**What to do.** Train new starters before they get access and everybody periodically after that. Cover phishing, passwords, reporting, and handling records. Keep attendance records.

**What OCR looks for.** Who was trained, on what, and when. Missing records for managers and clinicians are common.

**Have ready.** Training content; attendance or completion records per person; the dates.

### 164.308(a)(5)(ii)(A) Security reminders (addressable)

**Purpose.** Periodic nudges between formal training sessions.

**What to do.** Send something regularly: a note about a current scam, a poster, a screen message, a short item in the team meeting. Keep a copy of what you sent and when.

**What OCR looks for.** That anything at all happens between annual training rounds.

**Have ready.** Copies of reminders with dates sent.

### 164.308(a)(5)(ii)(B) Protection from malicious software (addressable)

**Purpose.** Guarding against, detecting and reporting malicious software - and teaching staff their part in it.

**What to do.** Run endpoint protection on everything, keep it updated, and make sure staff know how to report a machine behaving oddly rather than carrying on.

**What OCR looks for.** Coverage gaps: the machine in the back office nobody manages, or protection installed but not reporting for months.

**Have ready.** Endpoint protection console showing every device; update status; staff guidance on reporting.

### 164.308(a)(5)(ii)(C) Log-in monitoring (addressable)

**Purpose.** Watching for sign-in attempts that fail, and telling people about them.

**What to do.** Turn on alerting for repeated failures and unusual sign-ins. Decide who looks and what they do about it.

**What OCR looks for.** Whether anyone would notice a password-guessing attempt against your remote access.

**Have ready.** Alert configuration; examples of alerts reviewed and the outcome.

### 164.308(a)(5)(ii)(D) Password management (addressable)

**Purpose.** How passwords are created, changed and protected.

**What to do.** Set a standard - long passphrases, no sharing, a password manager, multi-factor authentication on anything reachable from outside. Say it in a policy and enforce it in the systems that allow it.

**What OCR looks for.** Shared logins at a nursing station or front desk. They break every other control you have, because nothing can be traced to a person.

**Have ready.** Password policy; system settings showing it is enforced; MFA coverage.

## 164.308(a)(6)(i) Security incident procedures

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** A plan for handling security incidents - and every incident is one, whether or not it turns out to be a breach.

**What to do.** Write down how an incident is reported, who decides what it is, who investigates, and how it is closed. Make sure staff know the reporting route.

**What OCR looks for.** An incident log that is empty. It reads as nobody reporting, not as nothing happening.

**Have ready.** Incident procedure; incident log with dates, actions and outcomes.

### 164.308(a)(6)(ii) Response and reporting (required)

**Purpose.** Responding to suspected or known incidents, limiting the damage, and recording what happened.

**What to do.** For each incident: contain it, work out what data was involved, run the breach risk assessment, fix the cause, and write it up. Link it to the notification rules if it is a breach.

**What OCR looks for.** The gap between discovery and action, and whether the breach assessment was done at all.

**Have ready.** Incident records showing discovery date, actions, outcome, and the breach decision with reasons.

## 164.308(a)(7)(i) Contingency plan

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** What you do when fire, flood, ransomware or a failed server takes your systems away.

**What to do.** Write the plan, back the data up, know how to restore it, and know how you will treat patients while the system is down.

**What OCR looks for.** Backups that exist but have never been restored. Ransomware cases turn on this.

**Have ready.** Contingency plan; backup records; restore test results; downtime procedures.

### 164.308(a)(7)(ii)(A) Data backup plan (required)

**Purpose.** Keeping retrievable, exact copies of electronic patient data.

**What to do.** Back up everything that holds ePHI, on a schedule that matches how much you could afford to lose. Keep a copy off-site or offline, so ransomware cannot reach it.

**What OCR looks for.** What is backed up, how often, where the copy lives, and whether anything is missed.

**Have ready.** Backup schedule and scope; successful backup reports; evidence of an offline or immutable copy.

### 164.308(a)(7)(ii)(B) Disaster recovery plan (required)

**Purpose.** How you restore lost data and systems after something serious.

**What to do.** Write the steps to rebuild each critical system and restore its data, with the order to do them in and who does what. Include the phone numbers you would need.

**What OCR looks for.** A plan specific enough to follow at 2 a.m. under pressure, not a page of intentions.

**Have ready.** Disaster recovery plan with system-by-system steps; contact list; last review date.

### 164.308(a)(7)(ii)(C) Emergency mode operation plan (required)

**Purpose.** Carrying on protecting patient data while running in an emergency.

**What to do.** Say how care continues when systems are down - paper forms, a read-only copy of records, break-glass access - and how those records are kept safe and entered later.

**What OCR looks for.** Downtime procedures that abandon security: paper notes left on a desk, or a shared emergency login with no record of who used it.

**Have ready.** Downtime procedures; break-glass account controls and usage records; how paper records are secured and reconciled.

### 164.308(a)(7)(ii)(D) Testing and revision procedures (addressable)

**Purpose.** Testing the contingency plan, and fixing what the test shows.

**What to do.** Test at least once a year. A tabletop walkthrough counts; an actual restore of a real backup counts for more. Write down what failed and change the plan.

**What OCR looks for.** A plan that has never been tested, or tests that always pass and never change anything.

**Have ready.** Test records with date, participants, what went wrong, and the resulting plan changes.

### 164.308(a)(7)(ii)(E) Applications and data criticality analysis (addressable)

**Purpose.** Knowing which systems matter most, so you restore them in the right order.

**What to do.** Rank your systems by how long you could function without them. Use that order in the recovery plan.

**What OCR looks for.** Whether the recovery order matches clinical reality rather than IT convenience.

**Have ready.** Criticality ranking of applications; recovery order in the plan reflecting it.

## 164.308(a)(8) Evaluation

**Standard.** No implementation specifications: the standard itself must be met.

**Purpose.** Periodically checking whether your safeguards still meet the Security Rule, and redoing it when things change.

**What to do.** Review your safeguards at least yearly, and after any significant change - a new EHR, a merger, a move to the cloud. Record what you found and fix the gaps.

**What OCR looks for.** Evaluations that stopped after the first year, or that never happened after a major system change.

**Have ready.** Dated evaluation reports; findings with owners; evidence gaps were closed.

## 164.308(b)(1) Business associate contracts and other arrangements

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** You may only let a vendor handle patient data if they have promised, in a contract, to protect it.

**What to do.** Keep a list of every vendor that touches ePHI - billing, IT support, cloud storage, shredding, transcription. Get a signed business associate agreement before they start, and keep it.

**What OCR looks for.** The vendor list against the signed agreements. Missing BAAs are a common and expensive finding.

**Have ready.** Vendor register; signed BAAs with dates; evidence the agreement predates the access.

### 164.308(b)(3) Written contract or other arrangement (required)

**Purpose.** The agreement itself, meeting what 164.314(a) requires it to say.

**What to do.** Use an agreement that covers what 164.314(a) requires - safeguards, subcontractors, and incident reporting to you - plus the terms the Privacy Rule requires in 164.504(e), such as what happens to the data when the contract ends. Do not accept a vendor's silence on any of them.

**What OCR looks for.** Agreements that are only a confidentiality clause, with no security obligations and no breach reporting timescale.

**Have ready.** Executed BAAs containing the required terms; a register of when each was last reviewed.
