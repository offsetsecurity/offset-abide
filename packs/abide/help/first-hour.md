# Your first hour

In order. Nothing here takes long, and everything after it depends on this
being done.

## 1. Add the people who will use it (10 minutes)

**Users** → add everyone who will record work here.

Give each person the smallest role that lets them do their job. Most people
need Contributor. Someone who only needs to look gets Read only, or Auditor if
they also need the audit trail.

A HIPAA programme run by one person stalls the first time that person is on
leave.

## 2. Turn on email (5 minutes)

**Settings** → your mail server details, then send yourself a test.

Without it, nobody gets reminded about overdue work, and an administrator who
forgets their password cannot get back in unaided.

## 3. Name your Security Official (5 minutes)

164.308(a)(2) wants one named person accountable for security. Not a
committee. In a small practice this is often the practice manager, and that is
fine.

Record it in **Policies** as a short appointment document.

## 4. Load the sample library (5 minutes)

**Settings** → example data. It fills **Assets** and **Risks** with the systems
and threats most healthcare organisations have.

They are a starting point to argue with, not an inventory. Delete what is not
yours, and add what is.

## 5. Open Get ready (2 minutes)

**Get ready** is the plan: six stages, 29 steps, in the order that makes sense.
It ticks steps off by itself as the underlying work appears.

Start at the top and do not skip the risk analysis stage. It is the first thing
OCR asks for, every time.

## 6. Check the backup ran (3 minutes)

**Backups** → confirm one exists, then copy it somewhere off this machine.

Everything you are about to record lives in this product. A compliance record
that exists on exactly one server is one failed disk from a bad afternoon.

---

Then read **Getting ready, in order**, which explains the programme, and
**The first day of a suspected breach**, which you want to have read before you
need it.
