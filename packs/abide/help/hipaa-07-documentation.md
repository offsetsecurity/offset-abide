# Policies and documentation (164.316)

This section turns the rest of the Rule into paper. You must have written policies and procedures for everything the Rule requires, keep a written record of every action, activity and assessment it asks you to document, keep it all for six years, make it available to the people who use it, and review it when things change.

In practice this is the section that decides an investigation. Work that was done but never written down cannot be shown, and what cannot be shown is treated as not done.

Part of [the audit-ready handbook](hipaa-01-how-compliance-is-checked.md). Previous: [Organisational requirements](hipaa-06-organisational.md). Next: [Breach notification](hipaa-08-breach-notification.md).

## How to read this page

Each **standard** is followed by its **implementation specifications**. Standards are always required. Each specification is marked **Required** or **Addressable**; see [the general rules](hipaa-02-general-rules.md) for what addressable asks of you. A standard with no specifications under it must be met as written.

The section numbers and titles are from 45 CFR Part 164. The wording under each is ours, not the regulation's.

## 164.316(a) Policies and procedures

**Standard.** No implementation specifications: the standard itself must be met.

**Purpose.** Having written policies and procedures that meet the Security Rule - and changing them when you need to.

**What to do.** Write them for your organisation, not from a template with another clinic's name in it. Approve them, publish them, and update them when something changes.

**What OCR looks for.** Policies describing systems the organisation does not use, or a template with the wrong organisation named in it. Both are read as never having been adopted.

**Have ready.** Approved policy set with dates and an owner; the change history.

## 164.316(b)(1) Documentation

**Standard.**

**Purpose.** Keeping your policies in writing, along with a record of the actions, activities and assessments the Rule asks you to document.

**What to do.** Keep them where they can be found: risk analyses, training records, incident records, access reviews, evaluations, agreements. Electronic is fine.

**What OCR looks for.** Work that was genuinely done but never written down. Undocumented compliance counts as none.

**Have ready.** The document set itself, with dates and versions.

### 164.316(b)(2)(i) Time limit (required)

**Purpose.** Keeping it for six years from creation, or from when it was last in effect - whichever is later.

**What to do.** Set retention to six years for everything named above, and do not let a system clean-up or a staff change quietly delete it.

**What OCR looks for.** Logs rotated away after 90 days; incident records lost when the ticketing system changed.

**Have ready.** Retention policy; evidence it is applied to logs, records and old policy versions.

### 164.316(b)(2)(ii) Availability (required)

**Purpose.** Making the documentation available to the people who have to follow it.

**What to do.** Publish policies where staff can reach them without asking, and point to them in training.

**What OCR looks for.** Policies in a folder only management can open.

**Have ready.** Where they are published; staff acknowledgement that they know where to look.

### 164.316(b)(2)(iii) Updates (required)

**Purpose.** Reviewing documentation periodically, and updating it when the environment or your risks change.

**What to do.** Review on a schedule, and again after any significant change. Record the review even when nothing changed.

**What OCR looks for.** Policies last touched years ago, in an organisation that has since changed its EHR.

**Have ready.** Review dates against each document; the changes made.
