# Using it day to day

A HIPAA programme is not a project that finishes. It is a set of habits that
leave a trail. These are the habits.

## Weekly, five minutes

Open the **Dashboard**. Look at what is overdue and chase it.

Look at **Findings**. Anything new? Anything open too long?

## When something happens, record it the same day

An incident goes in **Findings** the day it is noticed, not the day it is
understood. The date it was noticed is the date your 60-day breach clock
started, and reconstructing it later from memory is exactly the conversation
you do not want to have.

The same applies to a vendor telling you something. Note when they told you.

## When anything changes, ask two questions

A new system, a new vendor, a new site, a change of EHR, a move to the cloud:

1. Does the risk analysis still describe reality?
2. Is there an agreement in place before the data moves?

Both are 164.308. Both are cheap to do at the time and expensive to reconstruct
a year later.

## Quarterly

- **Access review.** Get the user list from every system holding patient data,
  check each account against someone who still works here and still needs it.
  Remove the rest and record what you removed. A review with no removals is
  usually a review nobody did.
- **Policy reviews that fall due.** Check the **Calendar**.
- **Test one thing for real.** In **Testing**: is encryption actually on every
  laptop, does the emergency account raise an alert, does a leaver lose access
  the same day. Record failures. Tests that always pass are not tests.
- **Look at Gap analysis** and close something.

## Annually

- Redo the **risk analysis**, properly, across everything.
- The **evaluation** under 164.308(a)(8): do your safeguards still meet the
  Rule?
- A **restore test** that actually restores data, not a backup report that says
  success.
- **Training** for everyone, including clinicians and partners.
- The **small-breach submission** to HHS, within 60 days of your year end.
- Export a dated report from **Reports** and keep it.

## Two habits that save you later

**Attach evidence when you do the thing, not when someone asks.** The access
review you ran in March is proof in November only if you kept the file.

**Date everything.** Evidence without a date proves nothing about now.

## What good looks like

An investigator asks for your risk analysis, your BAAs, your training records
and your incident log, and you produce all four in ten minutes, each dated
within the last year, each with a name against it.

That is the whole objective. Everything else in this product exists to make
that ten minutes possible.
