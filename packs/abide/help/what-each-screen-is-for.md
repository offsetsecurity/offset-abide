# What each screen is for

One line each, in menu order.

## The work

**Dashboard** — where you stand: requirements by status, what is overdue, what
is due next.

**Golden thread** — every risk, the requirement that treats it, and the proof, joined up in one map. Broken links show in red: they are what an investigator finds.

**Get ready** — the plan. Six stages from setting up to keeping it true, with
each step tagged with the part of the Rule it satisfies.

**Requirements** — all 72 items of the Security and Breach Notification Rules.
Set an owner and a status on each, attach the evidence, and read the plain
explanation of what it means.

**Applicability** — the decision record for every addressable item: you do it,
you do something else instead, or it is not reasonable for you and here is why.
The reason is the part that matters.

**Gap analysis** — what is not in place yet, in one list, so you can see the
size of the job rather than guess at it.

**Testing** — checking that a control you rely on actually works: is encryption
really on every laptop, does the leaver process run the same day.

## The registers

**Assets** — every system, device and service that creates, receives, stores or
transmits patient data. The risk analysis is only as good as this list.

**Risks** — what could go wrong, how likely, how bad, who owns it, and what you
are doing about it. This is 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A).

**Tasks** — the work itself, with owners and dates. A task can be marked as a
planned change, with what the change means for security.

**Findings** — incidents and audit findings. Every security incident belongs
here, including the ones that turn out to be nothing.

**Policies** — your written policies and procedures, with approval and review
dates. 164.316 asks for them in writing and reviewed.

**Suppliers** — every vendor that touches patient data, and whether the
business associate agreement is signed.

**Training** — who was trained, on what, when. Including the clinicians and
the partners, not only the front desk.

**Communications** — the security reminders between training rounds: what you
send, to whom, how often, and when it is next due.

**Escalation** — automatic reminders for anything with a date and an owner's email address,
one email each, 30, 15, 7 and 3 days before and then daily, and who is told, in turn,
as it goes overdue: the administrator, their manager, their manager's manager, top management.

**Interested parties** — patients, regulators, insurers, partner
organisations: who cares about your security and what they expect.

**Objectives** — what you are trying to achieve this year, and whether you did.

**Audits & reviews** — internal reviews and the management review that follows
them.

**Evidence** — every file that proves something, attached to the requirement it
proves.

## Looking back and outwards

**Calendar** — everything with a date: policy reviews, training, access
reviews, the restore test, the annual small-breach submission.

**Reports** — the export you keep, dated, so you can show what your programme
looked like at any point in the last six years.

**Audit trail** — who did what in this product, field by field. Administrators
and auditors only.

## Running it

**Users** — people and their roles.

**Settings** — email, branding, example data, the HTTPS certificate, and how
this product is configured.

**Backups** — the copies of your data, and how to restore one.

**Documentation** — these pages and the guides.
